PagerDuty woke me up at eight this morning. Very annoying. Later I found out there had already been an alert at five. I was sleeping so deeply that I must have missed it, and it probably escalated to my mentor. I imagine he'll be in pretty rough shape at work today too.jpg.

Close to noon, I managed to pull myself together and head to Beixiaohe Park. Still groggy on the way there, I let the bad mood spill over into all kinds of tangled thoughts: will AI take away our ability to think and use language? Humanity might no longer have to suffer from hunger, poverty, or even ideas ahead of their time, while the world turns into an ironic dystopia.

But after walking around the park and looking at the yellow leaves, willow trees, and sunshine, I felt a little better. The scene in my head changed too: if AI is this powerful, why not use it for quantitative trading and make a fortune? Deploy AI locally and let it mine factors around the clock. Make a little money, then go wild buying GPUs to scale things up.

Most of these ideas are naive, but there was also something I'd like to explore seriously in practice. Roughly speaking, the foundations of a system built with AI should be lean, well made, and thoroughly checked by a human. For example, use a strict language like OCaml, and first build and carefully review the entire backtesting framework underlying the trading system.

Then, moving up through the layers, let AI do progressively more of the work while gradually reducing human review. Rely on the automatic, rigorous checks provided by the lower layers to keep the upper layers in bounds, forming an inverted pyramid.

Lots of thoughts, not much practice. I need to change that.

Afterward, I went to China-Japan Friendship Hospital to see the doctor. That went smoothly. In the evening, I couldn't resist watching videos for quite a while, but I also got a haircut and watched a bit of a Jane Street video about OCaml on YouTube. At least I did something useful.

A fairly full day, all things considered. It's already 00:20. If I can get to bed within the next hour, that'll be pretty good!
