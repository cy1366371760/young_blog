Went for Chaoshan beef hotpot with a friend from middle school. He told me about a business idea: a creative workshop built around AI prompts, making money from things like PowerPoint presentations. I didn't see much of a future in it. There isn't much of a technical barrier to entry, and growing it would probably mean pouring money into promotion to bring in traffic. He seemed to be planning to get it going on a small budget, though. All I can do is wish him luck.

He also seemed very keen to move to the United States. My take was that people who haven't lived abroad often place enormous importance on democracy, freedom, and values. But once they move, they may not actually pay much attention to local government policies or voice the political interests of their own community. They may well continue much the same way of life they had in China.

Trading away familiar ways of living and cultural habits for certain ideals and slogans is a trade-off that deserves careful thought, rather than a decision to make in the heat of the moment.

If I consider changing jobs and moving to Hong Kong, or somewhere abroad, I need to think it through carefully too, and spend more time weighing the pros and cons.
