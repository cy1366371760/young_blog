Today, I had Korean food with three classmates from my language course in the UK. It was quite good: rich, cheesy flavors and a deep, rounded heat. I had a really good time.

We mostly talked about work, sharing our experiences and what we'd learned from going from campus recruits to newcomers in the workplace.

We also talked about relationships. J, one of the women in the group, asked T and me whether we'd date a close friend's ex-girlfriend. We both felt it wouldn't bother us, as long as we were a good match. J and L were both surprised: neither of them would ever date a close female friend's ex-boyfriend.

From this conversation, it felt like there were quite big differences between how men and women think and how they relate to their friends. Close male friendships might have more of that teasing, irreverent quality: casual, uninhibited, and more tolerant of each other. Maybe that's why dating a friend's ex-girlfriend wouldn't bother us much? Women's friendships might be more emotionally nuanced and intimate, with more attention to a close friend's feelings, making a friend's ex-boyfriend off-limits. It was interesting to learn about these differences in thinking and behavior.

After the meal, we took a group photo together. We only spent six weeks in that language class, yet here we are, still able to get together two years later. That says a lot about how precious those memories are, brief as that time was.
