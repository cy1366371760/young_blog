It's Sunday, and I couldn't resist again. Just like yesterday, I spent most of the day playing ARAM: Mayhem in League of Legends. Too much gaming takes a toll on the body, so, for the Nth time, I've decided to stop playing PVP games. While I'm at it, I want to watch fewer short videos and spend less time on my phone. Hopefully this attempt lasts a little longer.

I also want to start keeping a diary today: a record of what I do each day, so I can look back and work out what on earth I've been doing with my time.

Sometimes I feel that talking to myself or keeping a diary helps simply because it turns my thoughts into human language. Putting things into words can untangle them and help me make decisions I've actually thought through. When I'm caught up in a game or endlessly scrolling through short videos, that step barely happens. I'm just chasing dopamine and satisfying immediate urges, slipping into an almost animal-like pattern of behavior. Writing things down might help me regain a little perspective.

In the evening, I cleaned my room and finished Eileen Chang's novel The Rice-Sprout Song. Of course, I still watched some YouTube videos too.

The Rice-Sprout Song is very good. A great writer can capture the suffering and absurdity of an era while somehow reaching beyond it, almost as if predicting the future. That feeling stayed with me strongly after I finished the book.

On YouTube, I watched some videos about people who had fallen on hard times in Majuqiao. I felt sympathy for the people in those stories, but I can't say I truly know what it is like to be in their shoes. Watching them made me think about the lives of people drifting without a stable home and those working regular jobs. The former get by from day to day, with no settled place to live and sometimes not enough to eat, yet in some sense they are free. Regular workers have a different kind of stability and a different set of constraints. To some extent, the life we lead depends on the choices we make.

The job market is still bad, of course, but there are some opportunities out there. I need to keep trying. However bad this era may be, at least it is better than the one depicted in The Rice-Sprout Song.

That's enough for a first entry. I hope I can keep this up, so that when I'm an old geezer I can dig these pages out and reminisce.
