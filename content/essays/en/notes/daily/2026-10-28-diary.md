PagerDuty woke me up at eight again. I still felt pretty foggy throughout the morning.

Something I noticed at work: when people don't want to take responsibility for a task, they sometimes subconsciously come up with all sorts of reasons why it shouldn't be theirs. The reasons can all sound perfectly reasonable. But ultimately, handling it themselves might be the option that takes the least effort for everyone overall.

I think there's a lesson in that for me. When someone asks for something, my first, automatic reaction shouldn't be to think that the less I get involved, the better. Instead, I should consider what solutions I can offer. Compared with passing it to someone else, would handling it myself work out better for everyone in the long run?

After getting home, I watched videos for a while, then picked up CLANNAD again and played through two routes. The everyday scenes are still pretty funny, and the romance routes I've finished so far have all been good too. One thought it brought up about relationships in real life: if I actually want one, I need to say something and take action. Of course, reality is much more complicated. Is the other person right for me? Should I make the first move? It's not like a game where I can unlock every route and freely save/load to see what happens.

As for doing something useful, I started leafing through Real World OCaml again, trying once more to understand phantom types in the GADT chapter. I've read this material many times now, but I do understand it more deeply than I did on the first pass.

This time, I tried an approach that felt helpful: copy out the parts I don't understand, along with the necessary context, strip them down as much as possible, and think through the simplified version. I found that I could answer some of my own questions. For the parts that still made no sense, I asked AI to compare the original and simplified versions.

It turned out that a tiny change I'd made while simplifying was exactly the answer to one of my central questions. Roughly speaking, the type constraints in an OCaml interface can be stricter than those in the implementation.

This seems like a useful way to work through a problem. I'd like to try it more often!
