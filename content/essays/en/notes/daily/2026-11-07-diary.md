Sightseeing in Suzhou today: the Humble Administrator's Garden and Lion Grove Garden. The gardens were elegant, but very crowded. The little rockery maze at Lion Grove Garden was fun too.

While queuing, I spent a while thinking about the holiday make-up work system. Shouldn't it evolve into something where everyone can choose their own days off and travel at different times? In economic terms, that might also avoid a mismatch between when resources are available and when people need them...

Around noon, I passed Suzhou Museum. It was designed by some renowned architect, and the exterior was distinctive, but I couldn't get a reservation, so I didn't go in. Apparently, though, you can "rent" an older person at the entrance to take you inside: supposedly, people over seventy don't need a reservation and can bring a caregiver along...

For lunch, I had the erhuang noodles GPT recommended. They were awful, like gnawing on a dry block of instant noodles...

In the afternoon, I went to Pingjiang Road. A classic shopping street, though it did have a few tucked-away museums with fewer visitors. The Kunqu museum was quite good. It was screening an old Kunqu opera film, Fifteen Strings of Cash, which felt like a fine work even by today's standards. The performers were excellent. It also seems to have an important place in history; apparently, there's a saying about this one play saving an entire form of opera.

I had an early dinner at a place called Taohuayuanji: drunken crab, wontons in chicken broth, and small glutinous rice dumplings in sweet red bean soup. All pretty good. This GPT recommendation was decent enough.

Afterward, I went back to the hotel to laze around and keep playing Clannad. As I played, I found myself missing school life a little. Not that my own school years were especially eventful; things like a school romance still exist only in my imagination. But school life did come with more freedom.

I feel like I might finish Clannad tomorrow, and just thinking about that makes me a little sad. Still, that's a good thing too. Compared with the excitement, anger, and numbness of getting absorbed in short dramas and online games, the sadness and tears Clannad brings feel much more precious. They're also rarer in everyday life.
